# What Is IAM?

## Definition

Identity and Access Management, or IAM, is the combination of policies, processes, and technologies used to manage digital identities and control access to resources.

IAM helps ensure that the right individuals have access to the right resources at the right time for the right reasons.

## Main Purpose

IAM helps organizations:

- Prevent unauthorized access
- Reduce security risk
- Manage user accounts throughout their lifecycle
- Control access to applications, files, systems, and cloud services
- Maintain audit and compliance records

## Key Components

### Identity Lifecycle Management

Identity lifecycle management includes creating, updating, managing, and removing user accounts as people join, move within, or leave an organization.

### Authentication Services

Authentication verifies that a user is who they claim to be.

Examples include:

- Passwords
- Biometrics
- Multi-factor authentication
- Security keys

### Authorization Frameworks

Authorization defines what resources a user is allowed to access after they are authenticated.

Common models include:

- Role-Based Access Control
- Attribute-Based Access Control
- Discretionary Access Control
- Mandatory Access Control

### Access Governance

Access governance ensures permissions remain appropriate over time through reviews, approvals, and audits.

### Audit and Compliance

Audit and compliance involve maintaining records of user activities, access changes, and policy enforcement for security monitoring and regulatory requirements.

## Example

A Finance Analyst should only have access to finance-related systems and documents. Users outside the Finance department should not be able to access sensitive finance resources unless there is a valid business reason and approval.

## Key Takeaways

- IAM manages digital identities and access.
- IAM reduces unauthorized access.
- IAM supports compliance and audit readiness.
- IAM connects authentication, authorization, governance, and monitoring.
